Before you rewrite listings

Start with the visible gaps a buyer notices first.

Marketplace pages often underperform because the title is unclear, the image sequence does not answer enough questions, item details are thin, or the page lacks trust signals. Work through these checks before deciding whether the listing needs a quick cleanup or deeper managed support.

1. Search fit

Title and keyword clarity

  • Does the title lead with the product type and strongest buyer-use phrase?
  • Are important model, size, material, compatibility, or quantity details included?
  • Are repeated words, vague claims, and filler phrases removed?
  • Would a buyer understand the item without opening every image?
2. Visual trust

Photos and product proof

  • Does the first image clearly show what is included?
  • Do secondary images answer size, use, fit, texture, and package questions?
  • Are comparison, lifestyle, or callout images needed?
  • Would the image order reduce common pre-purchase doubt?
3. Page content

Bullets, details, and A+ / EBC

  • Do bullets explain benefits, use cases, specifications, and care or setup notes?
  • Are product details complete enough for filters and buyer comparison?
  • Does A+ / EBC content answer questions that bullets cannot cover well?
  • Is brand-page language consistent with the listing promise?
4. Buyer confidence

Offer clarity and trust signals

  • Are price, quantity, variants, shipping, returns, and condition easy to understand?
  • Are reviews, warranty, support, or quality proof visible where buyers need them?
  • Are there unanswered objections that show up in reviews or messages?
  • Does the page make the next buying step feel safe?
5. Account basics

Health, catalog, and operations

  • Are any priority listings suppressed, inactive, stranded, or missing key details?
  • Are catalog variations, item specifics, and category choices clean?
  • Are policy warnings, fulfillment issues, or stale promotions hurting performance?
  • Is someone checking these items weekly?
6. Priority order

What to fix first

  • Which 5–10 SKUs have the strongest sales or margin upside?
  • Which listings need quick title/detail cleanup versus full content work?
  • Which fixes can be handled in a 5-day sprint?
  • Which accounts need ongoing Amazon and eBay management?
Score your listing

Give each area a simple 0–15 score.

A score under 60 usually means the page needs more than a light edit. A score from 60–80 often points to a focused cleanup. A score above 80 may only need targeted testing or better account follow-through.

0–59Prioritize deeper listing cleanup before spending on traffic.
60–80Choose the biggest fixes and consider a focused sprint.
81+Look for specific tests, account-health gaps, or weekly management needs.
