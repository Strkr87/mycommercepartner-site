Marketplace growth guide

What to fix first in an Amazon or eBay listing sprint.

A listing optimization sprint is a short, focused project to improve the marketplace pages most likely to influence revenue — not every small detail across the whole catalog.

For many product brands, marketplace pages become uneven over time. Older products keep outdated titles, new products launch without complete details, photos do not answer buyer questions, and A+ or Enhanced Brand Content falls behind the current product line. A focused sprint helps improve the listings that matter most without forcing your team into a months-long catalog rebuild.

Start with the listings buyers already see.

Before rewriting the full catalog, choose the products with meaningful traffic, strong margin, repeat questions, weak conversion, or strategic importance. Those pages give cleanup work the best chance to matter quickly.

1. Choose the right listings before rewriting anything

The first decision is which listings to fix first. A useful starting set is usually 10 to 20 priority SKUs, selected from a mix of high-traffic products, underperforming products, seasonal products, and products that often create buyer confusion.

  • High-traffic listings: small clarity gains can affect more shoppers.
  • Important margin products: better pages can support the products you most want to sell.
  • Frequently questioned products: repeated buyer questions often reveal missing details.
  • Products with dated content: old bullets, weak photos, or missing A+ content can make a strong product feel stale.

2. Fix search clarity without making titles hard to read

Amazon and eBay titles need relevant search terms, but keyword stuffing can make a page feel messy. Strong titles usually lead with product type, brand, model or fit details, size or quantity, and the most important differentiator.

During a sprint, review each title for wasted words, missing fit details, unclear product type, and phrasing a buyer would not naturally search. The goal is not the longest title. The goal is a title that matches real buyer intent and still reads cleanly.

3. Rewrite bullets around buyer decisions

Bullets should answer the shopper's next questions in a logical order. Start with the main use case, then cover compatibility, material, size, what is included, setup, care, warranty, or other details that reduce doubt.

A helpful sprint rule: every bullet should either answer a buyer question, explain a meaningful advantage, or remove a reason to hesitate. If a bullet only repeats broad claims, replace it with specific product information.

4. Clean up photos, A+ content, and detail gaps together

Listing copy can only do part of the job. If the images, product details, and A+ or Enhanced Brand Content say different things, shoppers may still hesitate. A sprint should flag where photos need clearer angles, where comparison points are missing, and where product details should be filled in.

PhotosDo images show scale, fit, packaging, use, and important details?
Product detailsAre dimensions, material, quantity, and compatibility fields complete?
A+ or Enhanced Brand ContentDoes the page explain the brand, compare options, and support the main promise?
Buyer trustAre returns, condition notes, shipping expectations, and quality cues easy to understand?

5. End the sprint with a clear next step

The best sprint does more than improve copy. It should leave the brand with a cleaner priority list: what changed, what still needs creative work, which listings should be handled next, and whether ongoing marketplace support is worth considering.

If the first sprint reveals larger issues across catalog structure, account health, storefront content, or weekly follow-through, it may be time to consider ongoing Amazon or eBay support. If the sprint fixes the main problem, the brand may only need periodic reviews.
